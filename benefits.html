Benefits

Public benefits you may qualify for

As a refugee in California, you may qualify for several benefit programs. Apply as soon as possible — many programs have time limits that start from your arrival date.

⚠
Important: Verify eligibility before applying (2026)

Federal policy changes in 2025–2026 (including H.R.1) may have affected eligibility for some programs, especially CalFresh and SSI, for certain refugee categories. Always verify your current eligibility with your case manager or IRC/Catholic Charities before applying. This guide reflects general eligibility rules — your specific situation may differ.

Refugee Cash Assistance (RCA)

Monthly cash support for newly arrived refugees during their first 8 months in the U.S.

RCA at a Glance
Who qualifiesRefugees, asylees, Cuban/Haitian entrants, Amerasians, special immigrant visa holders
How longUp to 8 months from U.S. arrival date
AmountVaries by family size — typically $500–$1,200/month
Apply byAs soon as possible — time starts from arrival, not application
Apply atCounty HHSA office or through your resettlement agency
💡
Apply immediately — the clock is ticking

Your 8-month eligibility window starts from your U.S. arrival date, not from when you apply. If you arrived 2 months ago and haven't applied yet, you only have 6 months of RCA left. Apply today.

County HHSA: (877) 847-3663

Medi-Cal (Free Health Insurance)

California's free health insurance program. Most refugees qualify immediately upon arrival.

Medi-Cal at a Glance
Who qualifiesLow-income California residents — most refugees qualify immediately
What it coversDoctor visits, hospital care, prescriptions, dental, vision, mental health
CostFree (most refugees) or very low cost
How longAs long as you remain income-eligible
Apply atBenefitsCal.com or County HHSA office
BenefitsCal.com
Medi-Cal: (888) 777-0960

CalFresh (Food Stamps / SNAP)

Monthly EBT card for food purchases at grocery stores and farmers markets.

⚠
Eligibility may have changed (2026)

Federal policy changes in H.R.1 (2025) may have affected CalFresh eligibility for some refugee categories. Verify your current eligibility at BenefitsCal.com or through your case manager before applying.

CalFresh at a Glance
Who qualifiesMany refugees and asylees — verify current eligibility
What it providesEBT card loaded monthly for food purchases
AmountVaries by family size and income
Where to useMost grocery stores, some farmers markets
Apply atBenefitsCal.com or County HHSA
BenefitsCal.com
HHSA: (877) 847-3663

CalWORKs (Cash Aid for Families)

California's cash assistance program for low-income families with children.

CalWORKs at a Glance
Who qualifiesLow-income families with children under 18 who have work-eligible status
What it providesMonthly cash assistance + employment services + childcare
RequirementParticipation in employment/training activities
How longUp to 48 months (cumulative)
Apply atBenefitsCal.com or County HHSA
ℹ
CalWORKs includes childcare and employment support

CalWORKs doesn't just provide cash — it also includes free childcare while you work or attend training, plus employment services to help you find and keep a job. These supports continue even after cash aid ends.

BenefitsCal.com
HHSA: (877) 847-3663

SSI (Supplemental Security Income)

Monthly federal cash benefit for elderly, blind, or disabled low-income people.

⚠
SSI eligibility for refugees may have changed (2026)

Federal policy changes may have affected SSI eligibility for some refugee categories. Verify at SSA.gov or call 1-800-772-1213. Your case manager can help you check if you still qualify.

SSI at a Glance
Who qualifiesAge 65+, blind, or disabled low-income people — verify current refugee eligibility
Amount (2026)Up to ~$943/month for individuals
Applies atSocial Security Administration
Processing time3–6 months or longer
SSA: 1-800-772-1213

Other Benefits & Programs

Additional programs that can help you and your family.

WIC (Nutrition for Mothers & Infants)

Free Nutrition Program

Free supplemental food for pregnant women, new mothers, and children under 5. No documentation requirement for children. Provides food vouchers and nutrition education.

(888) 942-9675
Free

Section 8 Housing Voucher

Long-term Housing Subsidy

Federal rental assistance for low-income families. Waitlists are very long — apply as soon as possible. Administered by San Diego Housing Commission.

(619) 578-7584
Income-based · Apply early

Refugee School Impact Program

Free School Support

Additional educational support services for refugee children in public schools. Includes tutoring, counseling, and extracurricular activities. Connect through your school district or IRC.

(619) 641-7510 — IRC
Free

LIHEAP — Utility Bill Help

Energy Assistance

Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program helps pay heating and cooling bills. Apply through 211 or Community Services (SDG&E's CARE/FERA programs also reduce gas and electric bills for low-income households).

2-1-1
Income-based

How to Apply for Benefits

Three ways to apply — choose whichever works best for you.

1

Apply online at BenefitsCal.com

Apply for Medi-Cal, CalFresh, CalWORKs, and other state benefits at BenefitsCal.com. Available in multiple languages. Fastest option for most programs.

2

Apply through your case manager

Your resettlement agency case manager (IRC, Catholic Charities, or JFS/HIAS) can help you apply for all benefits in person, translate forms, and ensure you get everything you are eligible for. This is the best option if you are newly arrived.

3

Apply in person at County HHSA

Visit a San Diego County Health and Human Services Agency (HHSA) office in person. Bring all your documents. Multilingual staff is available at most offices. Call first to confirm hours: (877) 847-3663.

✓
Documents you typically need to apply

I-94 (Arrival/Departure Record) · Refugee Admission Letter · Social Security Number (if you have one) · Proof of address · Identification for all family members applying · Bank account information
