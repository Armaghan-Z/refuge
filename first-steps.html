First Steps

Your first 90 days in San Diego

Arriving in a new country is overwhelming. This guide breaks down exactly what to do — and when — in your first hours, days, and months here. You are not alone.

🌅 Day 1 📅 First Week 🗓 First Month ✅ 90 Days 📄 Documents ⚖️ Your Rights

Day 1 — Your First 24 Hours

The most important things to do immediately after arriving.

ℹ
Your resettlement agency is your first contact

If you arrived through the official refugee program, your resettlement agency (IRC, Catholic Charities, or JFS/HIAS) will meet you at the airport and take you to your temporary housing. If no one is there, call IRC: (619) 641-7510.

1

Connect with your resettlement agency

IRC San Diego (619) 641-7510, Catholic Charities (619) 287-9454, or JFS/HIAS (858) 872-0101. They will assign you a case manager who will guide you through everything.

2

Keep all your documents safe

Keep these in a secure place: your passport, visa, I-94 (Arrival/Departure Record), Refugee Travel Document, any medical records you brought, and your Refugee Admission Number (RA number). Make photocopies and keep them separate.

3

Get emergency food and essentials

Your case manager will help you get food and basic supplies. If you need immediate food, call 2-1-1 to find the nearest food pantry or food bank distribution today.

4

Know your emergency numbers

911 — Police, fire, or medical emergency. 2-1-1 — Social services and housing crisis line (24/7, multilingual). IRC: (619) 641-7510.

5

Rest

You have been through an incredibly difficult journey. Rest is not a luxury — it is necessary for your health and your ability to handle what comes next. Everything else can wait until tomorrow.

First Week — Days 2 through 7

Critical paperwork and health appointments to complete in your first week.

Day 2–3

Apply for a Social Security Number (SSN)

Your case manager can help you apply at the Social Security Administration office. You need your I-94, refugee admission letter, and photo ID. Processing takes 2–4 weeks. Once you have your SSN, many other applications become easier.

Social Security Administration: 1-800-772-1213
San Diego office: 880 Front Street, Suite 1225, San Diego

Day 2–3

Apply for Refugee Cash Assistance (RCA)

RCA provides monthly cash support during your first 8 months in the U.S. Apply immediately — the clock starts from your arrival date, not from when you apply. Your case manager will help you apply at the County HHSA office.

County HHSA: (877) 847-3663

Day 3–5

Apply for Medi-Cal

Free health insurance for low-income Californians. Most refugees qualify immediately. Medi-Cal covers doctor visits, prescriptions, and emergency care. Apply at BenefitsCal.com or at your county HHSA office.

BenefitsCal: BenefitsCal.com
HHSA: (877) 847-3663

Day 5–7

Schedule your Refugee Health Screening

Free health screening required within 90 days of arrival. Provided by San Diego County — includes TB test, blood work, vaccines, and referrals. Call to schedule as soon as possible.

County Refugee Health: (619) 692-8838

Day 5–7

Enroll children in school

All children have the right to attend public school regardless of immigration status (Plyler v. Doe). Contact your local school district to enroll. No documentation from parents is required.

San Diego Unified: (619) 522-1000
Cajon Valley (El Cajon): (619) 588-7691

First Month — Weeks 2 through 4

Getting settled, starting English classes, and building your support network.

✓
Start English (ESL) classes

San Diego Continuing Education (SDCCE) offers free ESL classes at multiple campuses. No ID required to enroll. Classes are available at all levels, including beginner. Call (619) 388-1800 or walk in to any SDCCE location.

✓
Open a bank account

You can open a bank account without a Social Security Number using your I-94, Refugee Travel Document, or passport. Recommended: Bank of America Opportunity Checking, Mission Federal Credit Union. Your case manager can help.

✓
Get a library card

Free at any San Diego Public Library branch. No ID required. Library card gives you free internet access, computers, books in many languages, and free English learning resources. San Diego Central Library: (619) 236-5800.

✓
Learn public transit (MTS)

Get a Compass Card (reloadable transit card) at the MTS Store (1255 Imperial Ave). Use Google Maps to plan bus and trolley trips. Call 511 for trip planning help. Low-income discount program (PRONTO) may be available.

✓
Apply for CalFresh (food benefits)

Monthly EBT card for food purchases. Apply at BenefitsCal.com or at your county HHSA office. Verify your eligibility first — federal policy changes may affect some refugees. Your case manager can help you check.

✓
Connect with your cultural community

San Diego has vibrant refugee and immigrant communities from around the world. Organizations like Alliance for African Assistance, IRC, and World Relief offer community connection programs. Meeting others from your home country can be invaluable.

Days 30–90 — Becoming More Independent

Work toward self-sufficiency and long-term stability.

✓
Get your Employment Authorization Document (EAD)

Refugees are authorized to work immediately upon arrival. However, you need an EAD card as proof. Your case manager or IRC Legal Services can help you apply. Keep this card safe — it is your work authorization.

✓
Start job search or vocational training

IRC's employment program, the County Office of Refugee Employment Services (ORES), and San Diego Workforce Partnership all offer free job placement help. Connect with them before your RCA benefits run out at 8 months.

✓
Complete your Refugee Health Screening (if not done)

Must be completed within 90 days of arrival. County Refugee Health: (619) 692-8838. This is important — some health issues need early treatment and are covered for free at this stage.

✓
Learn about your path to a Green Card

Refugees can apply for a Green Card (lawful permanent resident status) one year after arrival. Start talking to IRC Legal Services or Catholic Charities Immigration Services now to understand the timeline and paperwork required.

✓
Learn about driver's license (AB 60)

California law allows residents to get a driver's license regardless of immigration status. Having a license makes it much easier to get and keep jobs. Contact the DMV: (800) 777-0133.

Important Documents to Keep Safe

Keep originals in a secure place and make at least two photocopies of everything.

Documents from home country

Bring from day one
  • Passport (even if expired)
  • Birth certificates for all family members
  • Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  • School records and transcripts
  • Medical records, vaccination history
  • Professional licenses, degrees, certificates

U.S. immigration documents

Never let these expire
  • I-94 Arrival/Departure Record
  • Refugee Travel Document
  • Refugee Admission Letter
  • Employment Authorization Document (EAD) — when issued
  • Social Security Card — when issued
  • Green Card — when issued

U.S. benefit documents

Keep all letters
  • Medi-Cal card and letters
  • CalFresh EBT card
  • RCA approval letters
  • SSI or Social Security benefit letters
  • CalWORKs letters (if applicable)

Your Rights in the United States

As a refugee or asylum seeker in the U.S., you have important legal rights regardless of your immigration status.

Constitutional Protections
Right to remain silentAll people, any status
Right to a lawyerCriminal cases
Right to due processAll legal proceedings
Freedom from illegal searchesAt home & in car
Key Rights to Know
Right to attend schoolChildren, any status
Right to emergency healthcareAny status
Right to fair wagesAll workers
Right to report crimesNo deportation risk
⚠
If contacted by immigration officials (ICE)

You have the right to remain silent. You do not have to answer questions about your immigration status. Do not sign any documents without a lawyer present. Call IRC Legal Services immediately: (619) 641-7510.
