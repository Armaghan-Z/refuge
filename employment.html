Employment

Your path to a great job in San Diego

Refugees are authorized to work in the U.S. immediately after arrival. These programs provide free job training, job placement support, and vocational English to help you build a successful career.

✓
You have the right to work in the U.S.

As a refugee, you are authorized to work immediately upon arrival. Your I-94 serves as work authorization. You can also apply for an Employment Authorization Document (EAD card) through IRC Legal Services: (619) 641-7510.

Free Job Training & Placement Programs

These programs are designed specifically for refugees and immigrants. All are free.

IRC Employment Program

Refugee Job Placement & VESL

IRC's employment team provides individualized job placement support, resume writing, interview coaching, and job leads. Includes Vocational ESL (VESL) — combining workplace English with job skills. Among the most effective refugee employment programs in San Diego.

(619) 641-7510
5348 University Ave, San Diego 92105
Free

County ORES — Office of Refugee Employment Services

County Employment Services

San Diego County's dedicated refugee employment program. Provides job readiness training, employment authorization assistance, employer connections, and self-sufficiency planning. Works alongside RCA benefits.

(619) 236-6353
Free

SDWP Connect2Work

San Diego Workforce Partnership

San Diego Workforce Partnership's Connect2Work program provides free career counseling, job training, resume help, and employer connections. Available to immigrants and refugees. Multiple America's Job Center locations throughout San Diego.

(619) 228-2900
Free

IRC VESL (Vocational ESL)

Workplace English + Job Skills

Combines English language instruction with industry-specific vocabulary and job skills. Covers industries like healthcare, hospitality, manufacturing, and retail. Leads directly to job placement for many graduates.

(619) 641-7510
Free

Catholic Charities Employment Services

Job Placement & Training

Offers employment counseling, job readiness training, and employer connections for refugees and immigrants. Bilingual staff available. Part of their comprehensive newcomer services program.

(619) 287-9454 ext. 2100
Free

World Relief San Diego

Employment & Small Business

Provides employment services and micro-enterprise support for refugees wanting to start small businesses. Covers business plan development, microloans, and business coaching.

socalsandiego@wr.org
Free

Where to Find Jobs

Job boards and employer programs that actively hire refugees and immigrants.

Refugee-Friendly Employers in San Diego

Local Employers

San Diego has many employers actively hiring refugees and immigrants. Top industries: healthcare, hospitality, food service, light manufacturing, transportation, and retail. Ask your IRC case manager about the Employer Partnership Program — employers who have committed to hire refugees.

Ask IRC for current list

America's Job Center of California

Free Workforce Services

State-funded career centers with free computers for job searching, resume printing, and career counselors. Multiple San Diego County locations. Walk-ins welcome. Access to CalJOBS — California's free job board.

(619) 228-2900 — SDWP
Free

In-Demand Industries in San Diego

These fields have strong hiring rates and training programs specifically designed for refugees.

Healthcare Support

Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA), Home Health Aide

High demand, good wages, and programs specifically for refugees. CNA certification takes 4–6 weeks and pays $18–$24/hour to start. Training available through SDCCE and community colleges. Bilingual skills are a major advantage.

$18–$30/hour · High demand

Food Service & Hospitality

Hotels, Restaurants, Catering

San Diego's large tourism industry constantly hires. Hotels, restaurants, and event venues need diverse, multilingual staff. Food handling certification is free from the county. Entry-level positions often lead to advancement.

$16–$22/hour · Fast hiring

Transportation & Logistics

Delivery, Warehousing, Truck Driving

Delivery driving (UPS, Amazon, FedEx) and warehousing jobs have immediate openings. CDL (commercial driver's license) training is available through SDWP with potential wage increases to $60K+/year.

$18–$35/hour · Immediate openings

Construction & Skilled Trades

Carpentry, Electrical, Plumbing

San Diego's housing construction boom creates steady demand. Apprenticeship programs through unions offer paid training. Bilingual workers are especially in demand on job sites. SDWP can connect to apprenticeship programs.

$22–$45/hour · Apprenticeships available

International Credential Recognition

If you had a profession or degree in your home country, there are pathways to have it recognized in the U.S.

ℹ
Your foreign degree still has value

Many refugees were doctors, engineers, teachers, or other professionals in their home countries. While U.S. licensure processes take time, there are "bridge programs" and alternative pathways. IRC and Catholic Charities can connect you with credential evaluation services.

Credential Evaluation Services

Foreign Degree Evaluation

Organizations like World Education Services (WES) and Educational Credential Evaluators (ECE) can evaluate your foreign degree for U.S. equivalency. This is often required for further education or professional licensing. Fees may apply but some nonprofits offer vouchers.

Ask IRC for current voucher programs
Fee — vouchers available

San Diego Community College District

Accelerated Credential Programs

SDCCD offers fast-track certification programs in healthcare, IT, business, and other fields. If you have a foreign degree, advisors can help you get credit for prior learning and reach U.S. certification faster.

(619) 388-6500
Low-cost / free for eligible students

Workers' Rights

California has some of the strongest worker protections in the United States. These apply to all workers regardless of immigration status.

California Worker Protections (2026)
Minimum wage$17.00/hour ($20 for fast food) — higher than federal
Overtime1.5x pay after 8 hours/day or 40 hours/week
Paid sick leaveAt least 5 days per year — all workers
Workplace safetyOSHA protections apply to all workers
Right to organizeLegal to join or form a union
No retaliationIllegal to fire you for reporting violations
⚠
If your employer violates your rights

Contact the California Labor Commissioner's Office: 1-844-624-8217. You can file a complaint anonymously. Employers cannot retaliate against you for reporting a violation, and they cannot ask about your immigration status when you file a wage claim.
