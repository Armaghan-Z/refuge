Research & Sources

The research behind the design.

Every feature of The Refuge is grounded in research about how refugees access and use digital communication tools. These are the five sources that shaped this project.

Sources

Five studies that shaped this project.

1
"Design Better Communication Channels for Refugees"
What Design Can Do · 2016

This report examined how refugees receive and use information during displacement and resettlement. It found that refugees need accurate, accessible, and timely information — and that most existing communication channels fail on at least one of those three dimensions. Poor information leads to poor decisions about housing, legal processes, and employment. The design takeaway: a resource hub must be direct, link to authoritative sources, and avoid outdated content.

2
"Improving Digital Livelihood Opportunities for Refugees"
UNHCR Innovation Service · 2024

This UNHCR research focused on digital employment barriers — the gap between skills refugees have and their ability to enter the digital economy. Key findings: refugees face barriers in credential recognition, digital literacy, and access to job platforms designed for local job-seekers. The report validated The Refuge's employment page design: a plain-language job board, links to real platforms, and career development resources all in one place.

3
"Communication Strategy for the Rohingya Refugee Response"
Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) · 2023

This strategy for one of the world's largest refugee crises documented the importance of multimedia and multilingual communication to overcome language barriers. It showed that text-only resources miss large populations, and that providing the same information in multiple languages dramatically increases uptake. This directly informed The Refuge's six-language support commitment and its plain-language writing style throughout.

4
"Toolkit: Effective Communication to Meet the Needs of Refugees, Immigrants, and Migrants"
Vaccine Resource Hub / Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health

This toolkit provides evidence-based guidance on designing communication materials for migrant and refugee populations. Its core principle: messages must be culturally and linguistically relevant, not just translated. It emphasizes community-informed design, trust-building through familiar terminology, and respecting the dignity of the people receiving information — all principles directly reflected in The Refuge's tone, labeling, and content choices.

5
"Communication with Communities"
UNHCR (United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees)

UNHCR's operational guidance on community engagement stresses the importance of two-way communication in refugee assistance — moving beyond information broadcasting toward genuine dialogue and feedback loops. This validated The Refuge's contact form design (which asks for language preference and allows open-ended messages), its volunteer and mentorship section, and the goal of eventually making this a genuinely interactive platform.

Design Rationale

How the research maps to design decisions.

Resource Hub Structure
Sources 1 & 5 — Refugees need accurate, timely information in one place, with two-way feedback capacity. A single-site aggregator directly addresses scattered information.
Employment Page
Source 2 — Digital employment barriers require platform access, credential guidance, and career coaching alongside job listings. All three are on the employment page.
Six Language Support
Sources 3 & 4 — Multimedia and multilingual communication dramatically increases reach and usefulness. Pashto and Dari support reflects the specific San Diego community context.
Plain Language Writing
Sources 1, 3, & 4 — Culturally appropriate communication uses familiar terms and avoids jargon. Every resource description on this site is written for accessibility, not bureaucratic completeness.
Contact Form with Language Selection
Source 5 — Two-way communication is essential. The contact form asks for language preference so responses can be provided in the user's language, closing the feedback loop.
Sample Data Labeling
Source 1 — Accuracy is a core requirement. Prototype data (sample stats, sample stories, sample events) is explicitly labeled so users are never misled about what is real vs. illustrative.
About This Site

Prototype / Concept Status

The Refuge is a prototype concept site built as part of an HBS Project by Armaghan Zarak. All external resource links are real and verified — they link to actual organizations and government services. All San Diego organization details (IRC, JFS, Alliance for African Assistance) are real and accurate.

What is sample/illustrative: impact statistics (marked "Target"), success stories (marked "Illustrative Example"), sample job listings (marked "Sample"), upcoming events (marked "Sample Events"), team and partner sections (marked "Sample"), and contact information (marked "Sample").

The design, architecture, and research grounding represent a fully realized approach to what a real refugee resource hub could look like if built to production.
